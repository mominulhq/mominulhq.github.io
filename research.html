Research

What I work on

My research sits at the intersection of computer vision and clinical imaging: models that are small enough to deploy, and transparent enough for a clinician to trust. The three threads below are connected — each builds on lessons from the last.

Explainable medical image segmentation

MVT-Net

My MSc thesis introduced MVT-Net, a lightweight hybrid CNN-Transformer for retinal vessel segmentation. The design goal was to combine a Transformer's global context with a CNN's efficiency, while keeping the model small enough for practical deployment — and pairing it with explainability methods so that a clinician can see which regions of the image drove a given prediction, rather than treating the segmentation as a black box.

Test-time adaptation for medical imaging

In progress

Segmentation models trained on one imaging site or device often degrade when the input distribution shifts — a different probe, a noisier scan, a different patient population. This thread develops test-time adaptation techniques for echocardiographic segmentation, evaluated on public benchmarks including CAMUS and EchoNet-Dynamic, that let a model adjust to shifted inputs without full retraining.

Lightweight vision transformers for diagnostic imaging

SwinMedNet

A line of work exploring Swin Transformer variants for diagnostic classification tasks — starting with diabetic retinopathy classification on the RetinaMNIST2D dataset — aimed at keeping transformer-based models efficient enough for settings where compute is limited.
